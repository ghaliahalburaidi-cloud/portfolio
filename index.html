Featured Project 01 • Quantitative Risk & Generative AI

Stress-Testing an Investment Portfolio of Saudi Stocks

Graduation Project • B.Sc. Statistics, King Saud University (2026)

How would a portfolio of Saudi blue-chip stocks hold up in a geopolitical crisis?

Python • Monte Carlo Simulation • AWS (S3, QuickSight, Bedrock) • Generative AI
💻 View code on GitHub →

The Problem

Investors often use a standard “optimal” formula to decide how much to put in each stock. I tested how that approach performs in a crisis for a 1,000,000 SAR portfolio of four major Tadawul stocks: Al Rajhi Bank, SABIC, Saudi Aramco and STC.

What I Did

  • Built a Python model that chooses the best mix of stocks from three years of market data.
  • Simulated 10,000 possible futures for the portfolio under normal and crisis conditions.
  • Measured worst-case losses and tested the findings under many alternative assumptions.
  • Published the results to a cloud dashboard (Amazon QuickSight) and used generative AI (Amazon Bedrock) to write an executive summary, with every number checked automatically.

TO: Executive Management  |  FROM: Chief Risk Officer  |  SUBJECT: Stochastic Stress Test of Tadawul Equity Portfolio

1. Executive Summary

The stochastic stress test of our Tadawul equity portfolio under a geopolitical shock scenario reveals significant escalation in tail risk. The portfolio, optimized for maximum Sharpe ratio, shifts entirely to Al Rajhi under stress, resulting in a sharp increase in Value at Risk (VaR) and Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) at the 95% confidence level. The probability of any loss rises from 20.8% to 64.8%, highlighting the portfolio’s vulnerability to severe market downturns.

2. Allocation Shift and Its Cause

Under the stress scenario, the portfolio’s allocation shifts entirely to Al Rajhi, a decision driven by the negative expected excess returns across all assets. The stress scenario, which lowers expected annual log returns by about 25 percentage points, makes higher volatility more attractive in the Sharpe ratio optimization. Al Rajhi, with the highest stressed volatility, becomes the optimal choice despite its increased risk.

3. Tail-Risk Escalation

The stress scenario significantly escalates tail risk. The baseline VaR is 120,898.46 SAR, which increases to 475,530.92 SAR under stress, a rise of 293.33%. Similarly, the baseline CVaR of 171,438.39 SAR jumps to 535,819.93 SAR under stress, an increase of 212.54%. The probability of any loss also increases from 20.8% to 64.8%, underscoring the heightened risk exposure.

4. Source of the Additional Risk

The increase in CVaR under stress is primarily due to the shock effect, accounting for 75.3% of the increase, while the allocation effect contributes 24.7%. The shift to a stress-optimal corner allocation in Al Rajhi adds to tail risk rather than mitigating it, as evidenced by the higher CVaR under stress compared to maintaining the baseline weights.

5. Model Limitations

The model’s parameters are estimated from a limited three-year history, and correlations are held constant under stress. The simulated shocks are Gaussian, and while the model shows robustness with fat-tailed historical bootstrap shocks, it remains sensitive to the assumptions made.

6. Recommendations

  • Implement a tail-risk limit to constrain the portfolio’s exposure to extreme losses, referencing the stress scenario’s CVaR of 535,819.93 SAR.
  • Consider diversifying the portfolio beyond the current allocation to mitigate the risk of a corner solution under stress, as seen in the shift to Al Rajhi.
  • Review the optimization objective to include a penalty for high volatility or a constraint on maximum allowable volatility, given the stress scenario’s impact on portfolio risk.

Featured Project 02 • Forecasting & Business Analytics

Forecasting Coca-Cola’s Quarterly Revenue

Course Project • Team of Four

Can three decades of sales history predict the next two years of revenue?

R • Time-Series Forecasting • Data Visualization

The Problem

Companies rely on revenue forecasts to plan budgets, production and investor guidance. We analyzed Coca-Cola’s quarterly revenue from 1993 to 2024 to forecast 2025–2026.

What We Did

  • Explored more than 30 years of quarterly revenue and identified two patterns: long-term growth and a repeating seasonal cycle.
  • Built a seasonal forecasting model (SARIMA) in R that captures both patterns.
  • Validated the model by checking that its errors behave like random noise, with no pattern left to explain.
  • Forecast eight quarters ahead, with ranges showing the uncertainty.

Featured Project 03 • Survey Research & Statistics

Fear of Missing Out and Digital Hoarding: A Student Survey

Course Project • Team of Two

Is the fear of missing out linked to digital hoarding among university students?

Survey Research • Data Collection • Statistical Testing • Regression • R

The Problem

Many people keep thousands of files, photos and messages they never use, a behavior known as digital hoarding. We surveyed King Saud University students to find out whether it is linked to the fear of missing out (FoMO), and which part of FoMO matters most.

What We Did

Step 01
Define

Set the research questions and the dimensions of FoMO and digital hoarding to measure.

Step 02
Design

Built an online questionnaire from two validated scales and confirmed their reliability.

Step 03
Collect

Gathered responses from 300 students across different majors and study levels.

Step 04
Analyze

Tested relationships and group differences, then built a model to predict digital hoarding.
